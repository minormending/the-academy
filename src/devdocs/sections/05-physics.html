Player movement and physics

The player character is one class, src/entities/Player.ts, with three helpers in src/entities/player/: tuning.ts (every feel constant, exported as PT), NameTag.ts and fallbackTexture.ts. It aims at MapleStory's controls: you walk at a constant speed, jumps have a fixed height, you can drop down through thin platforms, and you climb ropes and ladders.

Some terms first. Arcade Physics is Phaser's simple physics engine: every body is an axis-aligned rectangle, with velocity, gravity and collision but no rotation or friction. A dynamic body moves (the player); a static body never moves and is cheap to collide against (ground, walls, platforms). A collider is a rule "separate body A from group B", with optional callbacks. Throughout the game, an entity's y means its feet: world Y grows downward, and player.y is the bottom edge of the hitbox, not the sprite's centre.

Body, hitbox and feet

Two constants in constants.ts fix the geometry: PLAYER_FRAME is 64×80 px (every character sheet frame), and PLAYER_HITBOX is 26×58 px. The hitbox is much narrower than the frame, so the staff and robe can overlap a monster without counting as a touch. It is centred horizontally and placed at the bottom, so the feet sit 2 px above the frame's bottom edge (the art draws feet at y = 78; PT.feetInsetFromBottom = 2).

  private applyBodySize() {
    const s = this.sprite;
    const body = this.body;
    if (!body) return;
    const fw = s.frame?.realWidth || s.width || 64;
    const fh = s.frame?.realHeight || s.height || 80;
    const w = PLAYER_HITBOX.w, h = PLAYER_HITBOX.h;
    const offX = (fw - w) / 2;
    const offY = Math.max(0, fh - PT.feetInsetFromBottom - h);
    body.setSize(w, h, false);
    body.setOffset(offX, offY);
    this.feetOff.x = offX + w / 2 - s.displayOriginX;
    this.feetOff.y = offY + h - s.displayOriginY;
  }

Source: Player.ts:858

The public position comes from the body, not the sprite: x is the body's centre and y its bottom (Player.ts:175). Code that moves the player calls placeFeet(x, y). It positions the sprite with the stored feetOff, syncs the body at once and also overwrites the body's prev and prevFrame positions. Without that last step, the one-way platform test below would compare against the position before the teleport and could let the player fall through, or snap them onto a platform they were never above.

Until the job's real sprite sheet has been generated (sheets are built lazily; see Art), the Player uses a 64×80 placeholder mage drawn with Phaser Graphics (ensureFallbackTexture). maybeUpgradeTexture() swaps in the real sheet the first frame it exists, and re-applies the body size while keeping the feet where they were.

Speed and jump are stats: readStats() reads progression.derived() every frame, so gear and buffs apply at once. Wind Boots, for example, add +20 speed and +30 jump. Progression floors them at 60 and 200 (stats.ts:84).

For a jump with no air resistance, the apex is v² / 2g and the air time is 2v / g:

  • Apex: 560² / (2 × 1500) = 313,600 / 3,000 ≈ 104.5 px.
  • Air time back to the same height: 2 × 560 / 1500 ≈ 0.747 s. At 200 px/s that covers ≈ 149 px horizontally.
  • Landing on a ledge 90 px higher: the feet pass 90 px on the way down after ≈ 0.51 s (solving 560t − 750t² = 90), ≈ 102 px of horizontal travel.
  • With Wind Boots (jump 590) the apex is ≈ 116 px.

Those numbers give the map design rule in ARCHITECTURE.md: one-way platforms that must be reached by jumping are stacked at most 90 px apart vertically (about 15 px of headroom below the base apex), and horizontal gaps are at most 120 px. Anything taller is connected with a rope or ladder. The rule assumes the base stats, so every map can be finished by a new character with no gear. See World for how maps are laid out.

Advanced: physics steps once per render frame

By default Arcade Physics runs at a fixed 60 steps per second, however fast the screen refreshes. On a 120 Hz or 144 Hz display, some render frames then contain no physics step and others contain one, so the player (and the camera following them) moves in uneven jumps. The QA pass fixed this with one config flag (commit 3ddfd19):

  physics: { default: 'arcade', arcade: { gravity: { x: 0, y: GRAVITY }, debug, fixedStep: false } },

Source: main.ts:29. With fixedStep: false, World.update takes exactly one step per frame, using that frame's delta. Phaser's game loop already smooths the delta to avoid spikes. The trade-offs, and what covers each one:

  • Frame-rate-dependent results. Integration with a variable step changes the jump apex by a pixel or two between 60 Hz and 144 Hz. The map rule leaves about 15 px of headroom for this.
  • Tunnelling on long frames. At the 900 px/s fall cap, a 50 ms frame moves the feet 45 px, far more than the 12 px thickness of a one-way platform body. Three things cover this: platformProcess's slack grows with fall speed, sweepPlatforms() catches crossings of up to 48 px, and embed recovery handles the rest (all described below). The Player's own acceleration and deceleration math clamps dt to 50 ms.
  • Order within a frame. Arcade steps the world on the scene's UPDATE event, before GameScene.update runs player.update. Velocities the Player sets are therefore integrated on the next frame, and collider callbacks have already fired by the time step() reads the contact timestamps.

The state machine

player.state is one of idle, walk, jump, fall, climb, prone, cast, hit, dead (the IPlayer contract in src/core/types.ts). The state is not stored and changed by events. It is recomputed every frame from a few flags and timers, in priority order:

  private computeState(now: number): PState {
    if (this.dead) return 'dead';
    if (this.rope) return 'climb';
    if (now < this.hitLockUntil) return 'hit';
    if (now < this.castLockUntil) return 'cast';
    const body = this.body;
    if (this.isGrounded()) {
      if (this.prone) return 'prone';
      return Math.abs(body.velocity.x) > 1 ? 'walk' : 'idle';
    }
    return body.velocity.y < 0 ? 'jump' : 'fall';
  }

Every state can go to hit and to dead; the diagram shows only a few of those arrows. isGrounded() means "not on a rope, not moving up faster than 1 px/s, and the body is blocked or touching below" (Player.ts:178). isBusy(), which the skill system checks before every cast, is true when dead, climbing, in a cast lock or in hit-stun.

The animation follows the state with a fallback chain (ANIM_FALLBACK): a sheet without a fall animation plays jump, then idle. While climbing, the animation pauses whenever the player is not moving on the rope.

Walking, jumping and air control

updateNormal() (Player.ts:425) handles everything except climbing. On the ground the horizontal velocity is set directly to dir × speed: there is no acceleration, as in MapleStory, so the character starts and stops instantly. Jumping has four helpers that make it feel reliable:

MechanicValueWhat it does

Air steeringairAccel 520, airSteerFrac 0.4In the air, a held direction accelerates vx toward a cap at 520 px/s². The cap is the take-off speed, but at least 0.4 × walk speed (80 px/s). With no direction held, momentum is kept.

A jump sets vx = dir × speed at take-off, so a jump from a standstill with a direction held gets full walking speed at once. Down + Jump never jumps: it either drops through a one-way platform or does nothing.

The jump latch

The QA pass (commit 5bc80f6, "jump latch") fixed taps that were lost. Comparing isDown with last frame's value misses a key that went down and up between two frames. The Player now listens to the Jump keys' 'down' event and keeps the press until readInput() reads it. Left and Right taps are latched the same way, so a very short tap still turns the character around:

    const pressed = (raw.jump && !this.prevJumpDown) || this.jumpLatched;
    this.prevJumpDown = raw.jump;
    this.jumpLatched = false;
    const tap = this.tapDirLatched;
    this.tapDirLatched = 0;
    // …
    if (pressed) this.jumpBufferedAt = now;
    let dir = ((raw.right ? 1 : 0) - (raw.left ? 1 : 0)) as -1 | 0 | 1;
    // A tap released before this frame still counts for one frame (turn around / nudge).
    if (dir === 0 && tap !== 0 && !raw.left && !raw.right) dir = tap;

Source: Player.ts:892. The keys come from core/bindings and are rebuilt when the player rebinds them; see Input, bindings and gamepad, which also covers the matching latch for hotbar keys. readInput() returns "nothing held" while control is disabled, the player is dead, or game.isInputBlocked() is true.

One-way platforms

A one-way platform can be jumped up through from below and stood on from above. The map loader builds each one as a thin static body whose only colliding face is the top (MapLoader.ts:238):

    for (const p of this.def.platforms) {
      const w = Math.max(1, p.x2 - p.x1);
      if (p.oneWay) {
        const z = this.zone(this.platforms, p.x1, p.y, w, ONE_WAY_BODY_H);
        const b = z.body as Phaser.Physics.Arcade.StaticBody | null;
        if (b) { b.checkCollision.down = false; b.checkCollision.left = false; b.checkCollision.right = false; b.checkCollision.up = true; }
        z.setData('platform', p);
      } else {
        // solid column from the surface to below the map bottom
        const z = this.zone(this.solids, p.x1, p.y, w, Math.max(32, H - p.y + 200));
        z.setData('platform', p);
      }
    }

Source: MapLoader.ts:240. ONE_WAY_BODY_H is 12 px. Solid ground is a column from its surface to 200 px below the map, so nothing can fall under it. Walls and two off-map edge walls go into the same solids group.

checkCollision alone is not enough. A player rising through a platform from below would still be pushed onto it when their feet passed the top face partway through a step. bindMap() therefore adds the platform collider with a process callback: Arcade calls it before separating the two bodies, and returning false skips the collision for that step.

  private platformProcess = (_a: unknown, b: unknown): boolean => {
    if (this.rope || this.now() < this.dropUntil) return false;
    const body = this.body;
    if (!body || body.velocity.y < 0) return false;
    const pb = bodyOf(b);
    if (!pb) return true;
    const prevBottom = Math.min(body.prev.y, body.prevFrame.y) + body.height;
    const slack = PT.oneWaySlack + Math.max(0, body.velocity.y) / 60;
    return prevBottom <= pb.y + slack;
  };

Source: Player.ts:611. The platform collides only if the player is not climbing, not dropping through, not moving up, and their feet were at or above the platform top (plus 6 px, plus one 60 Hz frame of fall) at the start of the step.

Down + Jump: dropping through

Down + Jump on the ground calls dropThrough() only if standingOnOneWay() agrees. That check uses timestamps written by the collider callbacks (onPlatformCollide and onSolidCollide, which record contact within 3 px of the feet). A recent one-way contact with no recent solid contact (both within 80 ms) means yes. Without contact data it falls back to map.platformAt(). A drop sets dropUntil = now + 250 ms, during which platformProcess ignores every platform, and gives a small 60 px/s downward push. On solid ground, Down + Jump does nothing, as in MapleStory.

The sweep safety net

sweepPlatforms() (Player.ts:644) runs after movement each frame while falling. It checks the platform data (not the bodies): if the feet crossed a platform's top since the last frame (by less than 48 px) while horizontally over it, the player is snapped onto the highest such platform and vy is zeroed. This catches the tunnelling a long frame can cause. It is skipped during a drop-through and while climbing.

Ropes and ladders

Ropes and ladders have no physics bodies. A RopeDef is just { x, top, bottom, kind }, and kind only changes how the loader draws it. The Player finds ropes by distance, through map.getRopeAt() with a fallback scan of map.def.ropes.

  • Grab with Up (findRopeAround, Player.ts:592): probes 12, 30 and 50 px above the feet for a rope within ropeTolX = 16 px whose top is more than 8 px above the feet. That last condition stops Up from grabbing a rope that starts at your feet. It works in the air too, so you can jump onto a rope.
  • Grab with Down while standing (findRopeBelow): a rope whose top is within 12 px of the feet, which lets you climb down from a ledge. The player starts at top + 4.
  • Climbing (Player.ts:501): gravity off, x snapped to the rope, Up/Down move at climbSpeed 140 px/s.
  • Leaving: climbing past the top places the feet on rope.top (counted as grounded); climbing down past the bottom, or onto the ground once 80 ms have passed since the grab, lets go. Jump + a direction hops off sideways at 0.7 × speed and 0.6 × jump velocity (140 px/s and 336 px/s at base stats). Jump with no direction does nothing. After any release, regrabbing is blocked for 250 ms (ropeRegrabMs), so a hop does not immediately catch the same rope.
  • No casting on ropes. playCast() returns early and isBusy() is true, so a spell tapped on a rope is kept in GameScene's cast buffer and fires only if you get off within 350 ms.

Up near a rope or a portal. Up is both the Player's up and GameScene's interact. player.update runs before GameScene.handleInput. If a rope is in reach, the Player grabs it first; GameScene then sees isClimbing() and does not enter the portal or talk to an NPC. While climbing, Up never activates a portal (commit 6492f47). Maps therefore keep ropes away from the portal reach window (40 px horizontal, 60 px vertical: PORTAL_REACH, MapLoader.ts:22).

Blink

Movement spells call player.blink(dx, dy). The contract (IPlayer) says it must clamp within the map and never end inside a solid. The target is first clamped to 20 px (blinkMargin) inside the map edges, and at least one hitbox height plus 20 px below the top. The player then walks from the origin toward the target in 6 px steps (blinkStep), testing the hitbox against every solid at each step, and stops at the last clear spot.

      // Horizontal blinks may climb over low ledges (hills / steps) instead of stopping at their face.
      const horizontal = Math.abs(ty - oy) < 1;
      let lift = 0;
      for (let i = 1; i <= steps; i++) {
        const t = i / steps;
        const cx = ox + (tx - ox) * t;
        let cy = oy + (ty - oy) * t - lift;
        if (this.overlapsSolid(cx, cy)) {
          if (!horizontal) break;
          let up = 0;
          while (up < PT.blinkStepUp && this.overlapsSolid(cx, cy - up)) up += 3;
          if (this.overlapsSolid(cx, cy - up)) break;
          lift += up; cy -= up;
        }
        fx = cx; fy = cy;
      }

Source: Player.ts:293. The step-up was added in the QA pass: before it, a horizontal blink into a 20 px hill stopped dead at the hill's face. Now it rises in 3 px increments, up to blinkStepUp = 64 px. A taller wall still stops it. Diagonal and vertical blinks never step up.

  • Only solids are tested. One-way platforms do not block a blink, so a blink can end in mid-air and the player then falls normally.
  • After the move the rope is released, horizontal velocity is kept and vertical velocity is zeroed, and the sprite fades in over 160 ms (blinkFlashMs).
  • If the origin itself is inside a solid, the search runs the other way: from the target back toward the origin, taking the first clear spot.

Hit reaction

Monsters and hazards damage the player through the combat system, which checks player.isInvulnerable() first and then calls player.onHit(fromX). HP itself belongs to Progression; the Player only handles the physical reaction:

  onHit(fromX: number) {
    if (this.dead) return;
    const now = this.now();
    if (now < this.invulnUntil) return;
    const dir = this.x >= fromX ? 1 : -1;
    this.releaseRope();
    this.prone = false;
    this.castLockUntil = 0;
    this.dropUntil = 0;
    this.lastGroundedAt = -Infinity;
    this.jumpBufferedAt = -Infinity;
    this.body?.setVelocity(dir * PT.hitKnockVx, PT.hitKnockVy);
    this.hitLockUntil = now + PT.hitLockMs;
    this.invulnUntil = now + PT.iFramesMs;
    this.tintUntil = now + PT.hitTintMs;
    this.state = 'hit';
  }

A hit cancels the cast lock and knocks the player off a rope. The spell that was cast has already fired (tryCast runs the attack at once), so getting hit only shortens the cast animation.

Embed recovery and falling off the map

Arcade separates overlapping bodies by pushing them apart along the axis of least overlap. That works for a body that just touched a wall. It does not work for one deeply inside a solid, which can happen after a teleport or a very long frame. The QA pass added a recovery step to checkFallOut() (Player.ts:701):

  • If the hitbox overlaps a solid for 3 frames in a row (not while climbing), find the highest top edge among the overlapping solids (solidTopAbove). If the feet can stand there without overlapping anything, place them there with zero velocity.
  • Otherwise teleport to the map's default spawn.
  • Independently, feet more than 100 px below the map bottom (fallOutMargin) also return the player to the default spawn.

Cast lock

The skill system calls player.playCast(lockMs) on every successful cast. The lock length is the spell's cast time reduced by castSpeed (see Skills).

  playCast(lockMs: number) {
    if (this.dead || this.rope) return;
    const now = this.now();
    this.castLockUntil = Math.max(this.castLockUntil, now + Math.max(0, lockMs || 0));
    this.castSerial++;
    this.prone = false;
    if (this.isGrounded()) this.body?.setVelocityX(0);
    this.state = 'cast';
  }

Source: Player.ts:244

  • The lock never gets shorter: a second playCast only extends it.
  • On the ground, horizontal movement stops for the whole lock. In the air the player keeps their momentum but cannot steer, and gravity still applies, so a mid-air cast follows its arc.
  • No jumping, rope grabbing or prone during the lock. A Jump pressed in the last 100 ms is buffered and happens when the lock ends.
  • castSerial restarts the cast animation for back-to-back casts, which would otherwise look like one long cast because the state never leaves cast.

Name tag and landing effects

NameTag draws the character's name on a dark pixel plate (2 px outline with cut corners) in the 16 px Kenney Mini pixel font, 3 px below the feet. Its depth is DEPTH.NAMETAG (45), above the player (20) and below damage numbers (50). updateVisuals() passes the name from progression.state.name every frame; setName() returns early when the name has not changed, so the plate is only redrawn on a change. The tag is hidden while the name is empty.

Landing after a fall of at least 170 px from the apex (hardLandFall), or at 760 px/s or more (hardLandVy), plays the land sound and a puff of seven px_smoke particles. Ordinary hops land silently.

Advanced: invariants and how to extend the controller safely
  • Teleport with placeFeet or placeAt, never sprite.setPosition. Only placeFeet keeps the feet offset and resets body.prev/prevFrame, which platformProcess and sweepPlatforms depend on. placeAt also releases the rope, zeroes velocity and resets the airtime tracking. Use it for spawns and portals.
  • State is derived, not stored. Add a new state by adding a flag or timer and a line in computeState() at the right priority, plus an ANIM_FALLBACK entry and the IPlayer['state'] union in types.ts. Do not assign this.state elsewhere and expect it to stick: step() overwrites it every frame.
  • Gravity is per body. grabRope turns gravity off and releaseRope turns it back on. Any new way of leaving a rope must go through releaseRope(), or the player will float.
  • Timers use scene.time.now (this.now()), which pauses with the scene. Do not mix in performance.now().
  • Prone does not shrink the hitbox. It only stops movement and changes the animation. Making ducking avoid projectiles would need a body resize and a matching feetOff update.
  • Collider lifetime. bindMap() destroys the previous map's colliders before it creates new ones. A collider left over after map.destroy() would point at destroyed static bodies.
  • Map authors: keep jump tiers ≤ 90 px and gaps ≤ 120 px, give every rope a top that meets a walkable surface (the climb ends by putting the feet on rope.top), and keep ropes out of portal reach.
