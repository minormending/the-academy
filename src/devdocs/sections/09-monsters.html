Monsters, AI and field bosses

Monsters are split the same way as everything else in the game: data says what a monster is, an entity class makes one live in the world, and a manager decides when and where they exist. src/data/monsters.ts defines every monster as a MonsterDef, with its numbers derived from the reference curves in src/core/balance.ts. Monster is the physics sprite with its AI, status effects and death. MonsterManager spawns monsters from the map's spawn list, respawns them, applies contact damage and answers "which monsters are near here?". DropManager turns a kill into loot on the floor, and Pip the owl (PetSystem) picks some of it up.

On top of the ordinary monsters sit two kinds of boss. Five field bosses, one per practicum map, appear on a timer, announce themselves with a banner, and fight with telegraphed attacks: a red-orange warning shape appears on the ground first, and the damage lands there a moment later. The Lich Archon is the final boss of the Arcane Sanctum. The Trial Hall's wave arena reuses these monsters but is driven by ArenaSystem; it is covered in Story.

MonsterDef and where the numbers come from

The contract (types.ts:301):

export type MonsterBehavior = 'walker' | 'hopper' | 'flyer' | 'stationary' | 'boss';

export interface MonsterDef {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  level: number;
  hp: number;
  exp: number;
  mesos: [number, number];
  touchDamage: number;
  def: number;             // flat damage reduction applied to player hits (keep small)
  speed: number;           // px/s
  size: { w: number; h: number }; // hitbox
  behavior: MonsterBehavior;
  aggressive?: boolean;    // chases player after being hit (always true for boss)
  weak?: Element[];        // x1.5
  resist?: Element[];      // x0.5
  immune?: StatusKind[];
  drops: DropEntry[];
  boss?: boolean;
}

Nobody types an HP or EXP number by hand. Each normal monster is written as a short MonsterSpec (id, level, behaviour, speed, hitbox, element matchups, its zone's etc item (a crafting/quest drop) and a few multipliers), and mk() (monsters.ts:80) fills in the rest from the curves:

function mk(s: MonsterSpec): MonsterDef {
  const lv = s.level;
  return {
    id: s.id,
    name: s.name,
    level: lv,
    hp: Math.round(refMonsterHp(lv) * (s.hpMul ?? 1)),
    exp: Math.max(1, Math.round(refMonsterExp(lv) * (s.expMul ?? 1))),
    mesos: mesosFor(lv),
    touchDamage: Math.round(refMonsterTouch(lv) * (s.touchMul ?? 1)),
    def: Math.round(lv * 0.4 * (s.defMul ?? 1)),
    speed: s.speed,
    size: s.size,
    behavior: s.behavior,
    aggressive: s.aggressive ?? true,
    weak: s.weak,
    resist: s.resist,
    immune: s.immune,
    drops: [
      { itemId: s.etc, chance: s.etcChance ?? 0.45, min: 1, max: 1 },
      ...potionDrops(lv),
      ...equipDrops(lv),
      ...(s.extraDrops ?? []),
    ],
  };
}
  • HP: refMonsterHp(lv) = 3.5 hits of the level-appropriate primary spell (refMatk(lv) × refSkillPct(lv) / 100 × 3.5), so a normal monster dies to about 3–4 casts. See Progression for the curves.
  • EXP: refMonsterExp(lv) = expToNext(lv) / killsPerLevel(lv), so a level takes about 8 + ⌊1.6·lv⌋ same-level kills.
  • Touch damage: refMonsterTouch(lv) = round(8 + 4.5·lv). Defense is a small flat number, round(0.4·lv × defMul).
  • Mesos (money): mesosFor(lv) = [round(3 + 2.5·lv), round(6 + 4·lv)], 6–10 at level 1.
  • Drops: the zone etc item (45% unless overridden), then two shared tables chosen by level band. potionDrops gives level-appropriate potions at 3–12% (red/blue below 12, orange from 12, white and mana elixirs from 25, plus elixirs from 40, with return scrolls at 3% from 12). equipDrops gives on-level gear at 0.5–2% in eight bands (below 8, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 45, and 45+), for example the five School staves at 1% each for levels 15–19.
  • Multipliers stay in roughly ×0.8–1.3 so monsters of one level feel different: golems are tanky (hpMul: 1.3, defMul: 1.6), flyers are frail. The earliest monsters run below the curve on purpose: a comment above MONSTERS explains that the starting 45% mastery makes an average roll about 0.73 of max, so reference HP would take 5–6 Arcane Bolts instead of 3–4.

Field boss matchups

Field bosses use a second builder, fieldBoss() (monsters.ts:192): HP is refMonsterHp × hpMul (28–65), EXP refMonsterExp × 18, defense round(0.6·lv), mesos [90·lv + 200, 140·lv + 400]. Their drops are a guaranteed trophy, a 22% unique accessory, a pile of 3–6 zone etc items, potions at 6× the normal chance (capped at 100%) and one or two more of each, and on-level equipment at 6× (capped at 50%). Their weaknesses are chosen so every School has a good matchup, as the comment above the list says:

// School matchups (each School has at least one favourable mini-boss):
//   fire → Shroom Monarch, Frostfang Alpha · ice → Magmaw, Tempest Wyrm · lightning → Frostfang Alpha ·
//   holy → Shroom Monarch, Tempest Wyrm · shadow → Magmaw. The Matriarch (lv 8) is fought before Schools exist.
Advanced: tuning monsters without breaking the curves
  • Change the curve, not the monster, when a whole level band feels off. Every normal monster's HP, EXP and touch move with balance.ts; hand-tuning one def's hp desynchronises it from the monster-hp chart and the reference math in Progression.
  • EXP and HP are independent multipliers. A tanky monster with expMul left at 1 is a worse farm than its neighbours; Magma Golem (hpMul 1.3, expMul 1.05) is deliberately so. Player EXP per kill is further scaled by level difference in Progression (killExpMultiplier).
  • aggressive defaults to true in mk(). Only Moss Snail and Puff Slime opt out; they get knocked back but never chase.
  • Drop chances are per entry and independent: a kill can roll the etc item, two potions and a staff at once. Keep new extraDrops small; DropManager fans every success out on the floor.
  • Ids are save and quest data. Quests count kills by monsterId, bounties are named q_bounty_<bossId> with trophy trophy_<bossId>, and maps spawn by id. Add new ids; don't rename.

The Monster entity and its AI

A Monster (Monster.ts:24) wraps one Arcade physics sprite. Its x/y are the sprite position, and like the player, y is the feet. The constructor builds the monster's sprite sheet on first use (ensureMonsterTexture), sizes the body to def.size at the bottom-centre of the frame, adds colliders against the map's solids and (except for flyers) its one-way platforms, and turns gravity off for flyers. Every frame update() clamps delta to 50 ms (so a background tab can't teleport monsters through floors) and runs tick() inside a try that warns once.

  protected tick(now: number, delta: number) {
    if (!this._alive) return;
    this.tickStatuses(delta);
    if (!this._alive) return;
    this.tickWorld(now, delta);
    if (!this._alive) return;

    if (this.isLocked()) {
      this.body.setVelocityX(0);
      if (this.flyer) this.body.setVelocityY(0);
      if (this.statuses.has('freeze')) { if (!this.sprite.anims.isPaused) this.sprite.anims.pause(); }
      else { if (this.sprite.anims.isPaused) this.sprite.anims.resume(); this.playAnim('idle'); }
    } else {
      if (this.sprite.anims.isPaused) this.sprite.anims.resume();
      if (now < this.hitUntil) {
        this.tickHitstun(now);
      } else {
        this.runAI(now, delta, this.speedMult());
      }
    }
    this.updateOverlays(now, delta);
  }

(Monster.ts:272.) The order matters: a burn tick can kill the monster, so _alive is re-checked after each step. tickWorld is a hook for bosses (their projectiles and warning zones keep moving while the boss itself is frozen). speedMult() applies chill: speed × (1 − potency), never below 0.1.

Edge checks are what keep ground monsters on their ledge. platform() asks the map for the walkable span under the feet (platformUnder, which tries map.platformAt and falls back to scanning the raw surfaces) at most every 200 ms and only while grounded. atEdge() (Monster.ts:355) then looks a little ahead of the body:

  protected atEdge(plat: PlatformDef | null, dir: number, ahead = 4, avoidPortals = false): boolean {
    const half = this.def.size.w / 2;
    const lookX = this.x + dir * (half * 0.5 + ahead);
    if (lookX < half || lookX > this.map.widthPx - half) return true;
    // Wandering monsters keep clear of portals so they don't camp the map exits.
    if (avoidPortals && this.nearPortal(lookX + dir * half * 0.5, dir)) return true;
    if (!plat) return false;
    return dir > 0 ? lookX >= plat.x2 : lookX <= plat.x1;
  }

Because the map's walkables are split around walls (see World), a wall standing on the floor counts as an edge too.

Getting hit: aggro, hitstun and knockback

takeDamage (Monster.ts:148) receives damage that CombatSystem has already calculated. It lowers HP, refreshes aggro, calls the onDamaged hook (bosses use it to wake up and enrage), and dies at 0. Otherwise it flashes the sprite white for 70 ms and, unless the monster is a boss or frozen/stunned, applies hitstun (280 ms during which the AI doesn't run) and knockback: a horizontal shove away from the attacker at distance / 0.16 px/s for 160 ms, so the monster slides the skill's knockback distance (at least 8 px). Hitstun stops the slide at a platform edge, and a new shove is only applied once the previous hitstun is nearly over, so a multi-hit spell doesn't launch a monster across the map.

Status effects

Skills apply statuses through CombatSystem.tryApplyStatus, which rolls the chance, checks immunity, turns burn/poison potency (a % of the hit) into damage per tick, clamps curse to 0–0.6 and chill to 0–0.9, and halves freeze/stun durations on bosses. Monster.applyStatus (Monster.ts:177) then stores it, one instance per kind: reapplying keeps the longer duration and the stronger potency. Bosses additionally cap freeze/stun at 2.5 s.

Only one tint shows at a time, in priority order freeze, burn, shock, poison, curse, chill, stun. Status particles spawn on a 90 ms clock. The damage side lives in two small methods:

  incomingDamageMult(): number {
    const sh = this.statuses.get('shock');
    const cu = this.statuses.get('curse');
    return (sh ? 1 + Math.max(0, sh.potency) : 1) * (cu ? 1 + Math.max(0, cu.potency) : 1);
  }

  /** Multiplier on damage this monster deals (curse weakens it). */
  /** Extra damage multiplier for this instance (Trial Hall scales waves above the zone's natural damage). */
  damageMul = 1;

  outgoingDamageMult(): number {
    const cu = this.statuses.get('curse');
    return this.damageMul * (cu ? 1 - Math.min(0.6, Math.max(0, cu.potency)) : 1);
  }

(Monster.ts:209.) outgoingDamageMult() is the single place curse weakens a monster. Contact damage, every field boss attack (FieldBoss.dmg()) and both Lich Archon attacks multiply by it, so a curse on a boss makes its telegraphed slams hurt less too.

Death, exactly once

die() (Monster.ts:655) is the only place monster:killed is emitted, and it is guarded so that happens once per monster:

  protected die() {
    if (!this._alive) return;
    this._alive = false;
    this.hp = 0;
    try { this.onBeforeDeath(); } catch (e) { console.warn('[monster] onBeforeDeath', e); }
    for (const k of [...this.statuses.keys()]) this.removeStatus(k);
    this.clearStatusVisuals();
    // …
    this.body.enable = false;
    // …
    try { bus.emit('monster:killed', { monster: this }); } catch (e) { console.error('[monster] monster:killed handler failed', e); }

Three systems listen: Progression (EXP and quest kill counts), DropManager (loot) and MonsterManager (respawn timer); bosses also listen to forget their dead adds. After the event the monster plays its die animation, fades up and out over 260 ms and calls destroy(). A delayedCall of 900 ms (50 ms with no death animation) finishes the job if the animation-complete event never fires.

Advanced: entity invariants
  • alive flips before the event. A listener that deals damage (say, an on-kill explosion) can call takeDamage on the dying monster; the !this.alive guard turns it into a no-op, so EXP and drops can't be granted twice. Burn/poison loops check _alive each tick for the same reason.
  • Listeners must not throw. The bus is an event emitter that stops calling listeners after one throws, and they run in registration order: Progression, MonsterManager, DropManager (the order GameScene constructs them), then any boss. Progression and DropManager wrap their handlers in try; the emit itself is wrapped too, so even a throwing listener can't stop the corpse from fading. A new listener should catch its own errors, or the ones after it (drops, respawns) silently stop working.
  • destroy() is idempotent and removes colliders, overlays, the HP bar, the death timer and tweens. MonsterManager.clear() calls it on map change for monsters that are mid-death, which is why the fade's onComplete and the timer both go through finish() and check destroyed.
  • No allocation in hot paths. boundsRef() returns one reused rectangle for per-frame callers (contact damage); getBounds() allocates and is for callers that keep the result. Never store the result of boundsRef().
  • Bosses don't get knocked around. takeDamage skips hitstun and knockback for def.boss, so their scripted attacks can't be interrupted by spam. Freeze and stun are the only way to pause them, and both are capped (2.5 s here, 1.5 s plus a lockout for field bosses).

MonsterManager: spawning, respawns and contact

MonsterManager (MonsterManager.ts) owns the list of live monsters for the current map. bindMap() (:74) runs on every map load:

  bindMap(map: IMapInstance) {
    this.clear();
    this.map = map;
    this.gen++;
    if (map.def.safe) return;
    // Build this map's monster sheets up front (one batch during the map load, not a hitch per first spawn).
    for (const id of new Set((map.def.spawns ?? []).map(sp => sp.monsterId))) ensureMonsterTexture(this.scene, id);
    (map.def.spawns ?? []).forEach((sp, i) => {
      if (isFieldBoss(sp.monsterId)) { this.scheduleFieldBoss(i); return; }
      for (let k = 0; k < sp.count; k++) this.spawnInGroup(i, false);
    });
  }
  • Spawn groups. Each SpawnDef is a group. spawnInGroup picks a random x in the range (clamped by half the monster's width) and spawns at the group's y, recording the monster's uid against the group index. Flyers get the range as their drift band.
  • Respawn. On monster:killed, onKilled starts a timer for sp.respawnMs (7 s for ordinary spawns; 3 min for the Lich Archon's Sanctum entry). When it fires, it tops the group back up to count with a 600 ms fade-in during which the monster deals no contact damage.
  • Choosing the class. create() instantiates the field boss class from FIELD_BOSS_CLASSES if there is one, LichArchon for any other behavior: 'boss', and plain Monster otherwise.
  • Contact damage. After updating every monster, contactDamage() checks living monsters that can touch (not fading in, not frozen or stunned, not mid-telegraphed-attack) against the player's bounds and calls combat.hitPlayer(touchDamage × outgoingDamageMult(), monster.x). It stops after the first hit each frame; the player's invulnerability frames after a hit stop the rest (see Physics).
  private contactDamage() {
    const s = this.services;
    if (!s.player || !s.combat) return;
    if (s.progression?.isDead()) return;
    if (s.player.isInvulnerable()) return;
    const pb = s.player.getBounds();
    for (const m of this.list) {
      if (!m.canContact()) continue;
      if (Phaser.Geom.Intersects.RectangleToRectangle(m.boundsRef(), pb)) {
        const weak = (m as { outgoingDamageMult?: () => number }).outgoingDamageMult?.() ?? 1;
        s.combat.hitPlayer(Math.max(1, Math.round(m.def.touchDamage * weak)), m.x);
        return; // one contact hit per frame
      }
    }
  }

Advanced: timers, generations and iteration safety
  • The generation counter. clear() and bindMap() both increment gen, and every respawn or field-boss callback captures gen when it is scheduled and returns early if it changed. clear() also removes the timers, but the check covers a timer that is already being dispatched during the map change.
  • Update iterates a snapshot length. update() loops over the length at entry, so a boss that summons adds mid-update doesn't update them this frame, and a map change that replaces this.list mid-loop can't break the iteration (destroyed monsters ignore update()). Destroyed monsters are pruned at the start of the next update.
  • Respawn counts living members, not timers: a timer only spawns if aliveInGroup(i) < count, so two timers firing for the same group can't push it over count. Monsters made with spawn() (debug, boss adds, Trial waves) belong to no group and never respawn.
  • Safe maps return early, so the campus never builds monster sheets.
  • The boss fallback is a trap. Any new behavior: 'boss' monster without an entry in FIELD_BOSS_CLASSES becomes a LichArchon (teleports, void-wisp summons and all). Give a new boss its own class, or use boss: true with another behaviour.

Drops and pickup

DropManager listens for monster:killed and calls dropFromMonster (DropManager.ts:52). Mesos drop 80% of the time from normal monsters and always from bosses, which split the amount into 4 bags. Every DropEntry rolls independently against its chance, with a quantity between min and max. The successes are shuffled and fanned out sideways (22 px apart, 28 for bosses), each launched 35 ms after the last.

  • Flight. Drops are plain sprites with hand-rolled physics, not Arcade bodies: launched upward at 380 px/s under 1400 px/s² of gravity, aimed so they land on the surface under their target x (groundBelow), clamped to the platform the monster stood on so loot doesn't rain off a ledge. They bounce once at 32% of the impact speed, then rest and bob.
  • Lifetime. 60 s, blinking for the last 6. At most 150 drops exist (MAX_DROPS); a new one past the cap fades out the oldest resting drop.
  • Pickup. Holding Z (or its rebound key) calls tryPickup() every 110 ms from GameScene, which takes the nearest drop within 50 px (vertical distance counts 1.2×). Drops less than 250 ms into their flight can't be grabbed. An item that doesn't fit in the bag is skipped so a meso bag under it can still be collected, and "Your inventory is full." is logged at most every 1.5 s.
  • Looks. Meso piles pick a coin by amount (bronze under 20, silver under 80, gold under 400, then a bag); items use their inventory icon scaled to 28 px.

Pip the owl

PetSystem (PetSystem.ts) is an auto-loot familiar drawn from a 14×14 pixel sprite defined in the file itself. Every 200 ms it scans drops.dropPositions() for loot within 260 px of the player, flies to the nearest at 420 px/s, and when within 22 px calls drops.pickupNear(x, y, 28), the same pickup path as Z. A drop it fails to take (full bag) is ignored for 3 s. If the player blinks or warps more than 700 px away it snaps back. It is toggled by the "familiar" setting or arcana.pet(), and GameScene clears and rebinds it with the map like every other system.

Field bosses

Each of the five practicum maps from Whisperwood to Stormspire Peak has one field boss in its spawns, with a 10-minute respawnMs. The data side (FIELD_BOSSES, monsters.ts:155) records each boss's map, the article for its banner ("A Shroom Monarch", but just "Magmaw") and an accent colour. The behaviour side is a base class, FieldBoss (FieldBoss.ts:88), and five subclasses in fieldBosses.ts.

When they appear

A field boss is not spawned with the map. bindMap hands its spawn entry to scheduleFieldBoss instead, which keeps one timer record per map for the whole session (not saved):

  • First visit to the map: it appears after fieldBossFirstDelayMs, 3 minutes.
  • Coming back while it was alive or already due: after 10 s.
  • After a kill: respawnMs (600,000 ms) × a random 0.8–1.2, so 8–12 minutes. The countdown keeps running while you are on other maps.
  • Level gate. When the timer fires and the player is more than one level below the boss, it checks again every 60 s instead of spawning, so new students aren't ambushed.

On spawn, the boss appears in the middle 30–70% of its span (away from the portals at the ends) with a fade-in, and announceFieldBoss (MonsterManager.ts:230) shows a banner ("A Frostfang Alpha has appeared!" over "Lv.24 field boss"), logs where, plays a roar and shakes the camera.

The fight loop

A field boss starts in roam mode, wandering its arena: the floor span under its spawn point, cut short by walls and by a 150 px keep-out either side of any portal (arenaAround). It also drops its collider with one-way platforms so dashes and leaps never snag on ledges. It switches to engaged when the player comes within 340 px horizontally and 300 px vertically, or when damaged: the shared top-of-screen boss bar appears and it starts choosing attacks. After 7 s with the player far away (over 1300 px across or 650 px up or down) it disengages and the bar goes away.

Each attack is a BossAction (FieldBoss.ts:20): a weight function (which can depend on distance), a cooldown, a start, and a tick that returns true when the action is done. Between actions the boss walks to its keepDist from the player, and when its action timer runs out pickAction makes a weighted random pick among actions off cooldown. Almost every attack works through telegraph():

  protected telegraph(rect: Phaser.Geom.Rectangle, durMs: number, onDone: (r: Phaser.Geom.Rectangle) => void,
    kind: Tele['kind'] = 'ground', dir: 1 | -1 = 1, color = WARN) {
    if (this.destroyed) return;
    const g = this.scene.add.graphics().setDepth(DEPTH.VFX - 1);
    this.teles.push({ g, rect, t: 0, dur: Math.max(200, durMs), kind, color, dir, onDone });

(FieldBoss.ts:205.) Every frame tickTeles redraws each warning in the same red-orange (WARN = 0xff3a2a) so danger always reads the same: a ground zone fills from the centre, a column (falling icicles, lightning) fills top-down, and a lane (a charge) fills along its direction with chevrons. When the time is up it calls onDone(rect), which usually calls hitIfOverlap(rect, this.dmg(mult)).

  A->>P: hitIfOverlap(rect, dmg(mult)) via combat.hitPlayer
  A->>A: optional hazard, projectiles or adds
  A-->>B: tick() returns true
  B->>B: endAction(), next gap from actionGap

Other building blocks: addHazard leaves a lingering area (slime puddle, spore cloud, lava pool) that ticks damage while you stand in it, with defense mitigation but no knockback or invulnerability frames; addProjectile lobs something that can hit directly or call onLand; makeCharge builds a lane telegraph followed by a dash to the far end of the arena that hits once; makeSummon calls adds up to maxAdds. While a dash or leap is in progress noContact is set, so the body itself doesn't also deal contact damage: the telegraphed hit is the damage.

Advanced: fairness rules and failure modes in boss code
  • No chain-locking. FieldBoss.applyStatus caps freeze/stun at 1.5 s (after CombatSystem has already halved it) and then refuses any freeze/stun for that duration plus 4 s. A lightning or ice build can interrupt a boss but not lock it down for the whole fight.
  • Telegraphs keep resolving while the boss is frozen, because they tick in tickWorld, which runs even when locked. Freezing a boss mid-attack does not cancel the zone already on the floor.
  • The player's death can destroy the boss mid-loop. In the Trial Hall a lethal hit ends the run and despawns the boss from inside onDone. tickTeles and tickHazards iterate a copy (slice()) and check destroyed each step; addHazard and addProjectile destroy their images immediately if the boss is already gone.
  • Hazard damage is fixed at creation. addHazard receives this.dmg(0.15) when the puddle appears, so a curse applied later does not weaken puddles already on the floor; new attacks do pick it up.
  • Arena clamping. clampX keeps every target point inside the arena, and charges end at the arena edge or on a wall bump, so a boss can't follow the player through a portal or off its floor. If the arena would be under 200 px wide it falls back to ±100 px around the spawn.
  • Adding an attack. Return a BossAction from a method and add it to this.actions in the constructor. Route every damage number through this.dmg(mult) (so curse and the Trial Hall's damageMul apply), telegraph for 0.6–1 s, and give the action a cancel if it sets velocity or noContact.
  • Timers are session state. fieldBossTimers() reports each map's countdown, and fieldBossFirstDelayMs is a public field you can shorten from the console (arcana.s.monsters.fieldBossFirstDelayMs = 5000) before entering a map to test a boss.

The Lich Archon

LichArchon (LichArchon.ts:19) predates the field-boss base class and has its own small state machine: dormant, idle, volley, slam, teleport and summon. It wakes when the player comes within 640 px horizontally and 320 px vertically, or when hit, and shows its boss bar. In idle it walks toward the player until within 170 px, and when its action timer runs out it picks:

The Lich does not override applyStatus, so it gets the base boss rule (freeze/stun halved by combat, then capped at 2.5 s) without the field bosses' 4 s lockout.
